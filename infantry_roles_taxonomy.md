# Infantry Roles Taxonomy

## Derived from the Infantry Identity Framework (7 Factors × 4 Faces × 5 Modes × 7 Levels)

The source framework defines **who a soldier is** — personality, identity, psychological orientation along seven axes. This taxonomy defines **what a soldier does** — the seven functional jobs that constitute a unit's operational capability. Any soldier, regardless of which source factor dominates their identity, can occupy any of these roles. A Linked can be a Breacher. A Spent can be a Caller. The role is the job; the factor is the person doing it.

---

## Role 1: Breacher ↔ Warden — *Threshold Control*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages contested access points — opening spaces the formation needs to enter or sealing spaces the formation needs to deny. Every engagement has a moment where passage is either forced or forbidden; this role owns that moment.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover what happens inside the space once accessed (that's the Steward ↔ Harvester), the decision of *which* threshold to target (that's the Caller ↔ Executor), or the detection of threats beyond the threshold (that's the Feeler ↔ Curtain). The Breacher ↔ Warden operates the door. They don't choose the door, fight the room, or watch the street.

### Faces

**1. The Key**
*"I read the threshold and the threshold tells me how it opens. Every barrier has a seam. My job is finding seams, not making holes."*
- **Defining Capability:** The bypass kit — electronic intrusion tools, mechanical picks, social engineering protocols. The capability that finds how a barrier was designed to open and opens it that way, without alerting the architecture on the other side.

**2. The Ram**
*"When the seam won't give, I make a new one. The wall becomes the door. Speed is access and I am very fast."*
- **Defining Capability:** The breaching charge — directional explosive, ballistic ram, kinetic entry package. The tool that creates openings in things that were built to have none, trading subtlety for certainty.

**3. The Lock**
*"I read what the enemy needs to reach and I seal it. Every approach has a chokepoint. I make the chokepoint absolute."*
- **Defining Capability:** The obstacle system — mines, barriers, denial munitions, area-denial weapons. The tools that convert open ground into sealed ground and keep it sealed under pressure.

**4. The Vault**
*"Nothing passes this point. Not through ingenuity, not through force, not through patience. The space behind me is closed by my presence and my presence does not move."*
- **Defining Capability:** The hardened position — the fighting hole deepened to fortification, the chokepoint held by a soldier who has made the denial personal. The barrier is the soldier and the soldier is the barrier.

### Modes

1. **Assessment** — How the Breacher ↔ Warden reads the threshold before committing: structural composition, enemy preparation on the far side, timing windows, and the fundamental question of whether to open or seal.
2. **Execution** — The moment of transition: the charge detonates, the bypass engages, the barrier locks into place. The irreversible instant where "closed" becomes "open" or "open" becomes "denied."
3. **Contested Threshold** — How the role performs when the enemy is actively fighting for control of the same access point: breaching against prepared defense, or holding a barrier under direct assault.
4. **Handoff** — How the Breacher ↔ Warden transitions the threshold to the next function: the breach that becomes a corridor for the formation, the sealed barrier that becomes the Warden's permanent position for the Curtain to observe beyond.
5. **Reset** — How the role reconstitutes for the next threshold: reloading breaching capability, repositioning denial systems, reading the next barrier while the dust from the current one is still settling.

## Role 2: Feeler ↔ Curtain — *Awareness Boundary*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages the formation's perceptual edge — pushing awareness outward to detect threats and opportunities before they mature, or maintaining an established detection perimeter that nothing crosses unidentified. This role defines how far the formation can see and how trustworthy that seeing is.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover the kinetic response to detected threats (that's the Striker ↔ Absorber), the movement of the formation through detected terrain (that's the Paver ↔ Root), or the management of information systems that carry what's detected (that's the Relay ↔ Veil). The Feeler ↔ Curtain finds and watches. They don't shoot, route, or transmit.

### Faces

**1. The Whisker**
*"I move ahead of the formation because the formation needs to know what's coming before it arrives. My body is the first question the environment gets asked."*
- **Defining Capability:** The concealed observation position — the hide site that sees without being seen, reporting contact before contact becomes engagement. The scout's oldest tool: presence without signature.

**2. The Hound**
*"I don't wait for threats to wander into our perimeter. I go where threats breed and I flush them into the open before they're ready. Scouting is hunting without the kill."*
- **Defining Capability:** The patrol route — the deliberate path through threat-likely terrain, designed not to avoid danger but to find it early, on the Hound's terms rather than the enemy's.

**3. The Post**
*"I stand at the perimeter and the perimeter holds because I hold it. What approaches is identified. What I can't identify doesn't pass. The line is me."*
- **Defining Capability:** The challenge protocol and the weapon behind it — the combination of identification procedure and immediate engagement capability that converts every approach into a binary: known or stopped.

**4. The Web**
*"I don't guard a line. I guard a system. Sensors, overlapping positions, redundant coverage — the perimeter is an architecture, and I am its architect."*
- **Defining Capability:** The integrated sensor array — observation posts, electronic detection devices, early-warning systems layered into a network where no single failure creates a gap.

### Modes

1. **Detection** — How the Feeler ↔ Curtain first acquires anomaly: the initial read on movement, signal, absence, or wrongness that says something in the environment has changed.
2. **Classification** — Converting raw detection into actionable identity: friendly, hostile, civilian, unknown, decoy. The judgment that determines whether the next action is a report or a trigger pull.
3. **Reporting** — How the role communicates what it has found: translating perception into format the formation can act on, at the speed the formation needs to act.
4. **Endurance** — How the role sustains alertness across time: the long watch, the repeated patrol, the grinding requirement to remain perceptive when fatigue, routine, and sensory adaptation erode the ability to notice.
5. **Compromise** — How the Feeler ↔ Curtain responds when their own position is detected: the scout who's been made, the sentry whose post is targeted. The moment the watcher becomes the watched.

## Role 3: Striker ↔ Absorber — *Violence Vector*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages the direction kinetic force travels — projecting fires that destroy or suppress the enemy's ability to act, or receiving and surviving fires to protect the formation behind them. This role is the barrel and the shield: it decides whether violence flows outward or breaks against the position.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover the decision of *when* to fire or *what* to fire at (that's the Caller ↔ Executor), the management of contested access points where fire is concentrated (that's the Breacher ↔ Warden), or the sustainment of the formation's ability to keep fighting (that's the Steward ↔ Harvester). The Striker ↔ Absorber delivers and endures violence. They don't authorize it, doorway it, or resupply it.

### Faces

**1. The Hammer**
*"I deliver the effect that ends the fight. When I fire, the enemy's position becomes a former position. Suppression buys time. Destruction buys victory."*
- **Defining Capability:** The heavy weapon system — crew-served, vehicle-mounted, or suit-integrated. The fire capability that doesn't merely suppress but destroys, converting enemy combat power into wreckage.

**2. The Needle**
*"I don't saturate. I select. One target, one solution, one round. Precision is more violent than volume because precision cannot be answered with cover."*
- **Defining Capability:** The precision engagement system — the marksman's rifle, the guided munition, the single shot placed exactly where the enemy's capability is most fragile.

**3. The Rampart**
*"I absorb what the enemy throws and the formation behind me doesn't feel it. My armor, my position, my willingness to stand in the beaten zone — the squad's survival lives on my back."*
- **Defining Capability:** The armored position — the combination of physical protection (suit, vehicle, fortification) and terrain that converts incoming fire from lethal to survivable, bought with the Rampart's willingness to be the thing that gets hit.

**4. The Smoke**
*"I don't block the fire. I make the fire miss. Concealment, deception, displacement — the enemy fires at where we were, not where we are, and the miss is my product."*
- **Defining Capability:** The obscuration and deception package — smoke, decoys, false thermal signatures, electronic spoofing. The tools that protect by misdirection rather than absorption.

### Modes

1. **Acquisition** — How the Striker ↔ Absorber identifies what to engage or what to shield against: target recognition, threat prioritization, the read on where violence needs to go or where it's coming from.
2. **Application** — The active phase: fire delivered or fire absorbed. Rounds downrange or impacts weathered. The moment the role is performing its core function.
3. **Transition** — Shifting between offense and defense within the same engagement: the striker who must suddenly absorb return fire, the absorber who must suddenly project fires to relieve pressure. The role's agility under contact.
4. **Sustainment** — Maintaining capability across extended contact: ammunition discipline, armor integrity, physical endurance, the logistics of prolonged violence and the judgment to know when the supply is running critical.
5. **Overshoot** — How the role recognizes when force has exceeded purpose: the striker still firing after the threat is neutralized, the absorber still absorbing when displacement is the correct answer. The discipline of enough.

## Role 4: Relay ↔ Veil — *Signal Architecture*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages the formation's information infrastructure — ensuring the right data reaches the right person at the right time, or denying the enemy that same capability. This role determines whether the formation fights as a coordinated organism or as isolated nodes, and whether the enemy does either.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover the content of decisions made with information (that's the Caller ↔ Executor), the physical detection of threats that generate information (that's the Feeler ↔ Curtain), or the human-machine integration that processes information (that's the source framework's System × Flesh factor — identity, not job). The Relay ↔ Veil builds and attacks pipes. They don't generate the water or decide where it goes.

### Faces

**1. The Bridge**
*"I connect the parts of the formation that can't hear each other. When the order reaches the fire team on the far flank, it traveled through me. I am the formation's nervous system."*
- **Defining Capability:** The communications relay — the equipment, positioning, and judgment that keeps the tactical network alive when terrain, distance, or jamming would fracture it.

**2. The Filter**
*"Not everything needs to reach everyone. I manage what goes where — the commander gets the threat summary, the medic gets the casualty nine-line, the rifleman gets the fire command. Nobody gets the noise."*
- **Defining Capability:** The information management protocol — the discipline that converts the raw data firehose into curated streams matched to each recipient's decision needs.

**3. The Ghost**
*"The enemy's sensors see nothing because I made us invisible. Their comms carry static because I filled them with it. Their picture of the battlespace is a painting I made."*
- **Defining Capability:** The electronic warfare suite — jamming, spoofing, signal interception, emissions control. The capability that degrades, denies, and deceives the enemy's information systems.

**4. The Mirror**
*"I show the enemy what I want them to see. Their intelligence is my product. Their assessment is my design. They plan against a formation that exists only in their sensors."*
- **Defining Capability:** The deception package — false emissions, decoy signatures, spoofed network traffic, planted signals. The tools that create an information environment the enemy trusts and shouldn't.

### Modes

1. **Architecture** — How the Relay ↔ Veil designs and maintains the formation's information network: topology, redundancy paths, fallback protocols, the structure that keeps data flowing when pieces of the network are destroyed.
2. **Translation** — Converting raw data into formats recipients can act on at the speed they need to act: the sensor return that becomes a fire command, the intercept that becomes a route change.
3. **Denial** — Actively degrading the enemy's information capability: jamming their nets, intercepting their traffic, spoofing their sensors, ensuring that the enemy's version of the battlespace is incomplete, delayed, or wrong.
4. **Degraded Operations** — How the role functions when the formation's own network is under attack: maintaining information flow through contested electromagnetic space, falling back to analog when digital fails.
5. **Integrity** — How the role ensures that what the formation receives is true: detecting enemy injection into friendly networks, verifying data provenance, maintaining trust in the information the formation fights on.

## Role 5: Steward ↔ Harvester — *Continuity of Capability*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages the formation's ability to keep fighting — preserving combat power through medical treatment, resupply, and repair, or recovering usable capability from the battlefield's wreckage when organic supply fails. This role defines whether the formation can fight tomorrow or only today.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover the conduct of the current fight (that's the Striker ↔ Absorber), the decision of where and when to fight (that's the Caller ↔ Executor), or the detection of what the formation is fighting (that's the Feeler ↔ Curtain). The Steward ↔ Harvester keeps the machine running. They don't aim it, position it, or identify its targets.

### Faces

**1. The Mender**
*"Broken things are my jurisdiction — soldiers, equipment, positions. I restore what the fight degrades. The formation's yesterday is my blueprint for its tomorrow."*
- **Defining Capability:** The aid bag and the repair kit — the dual capability that treats casualties and patches equipment with the same professional urgency, because a wounded soldier and a disabled weapon system are the same problem: degraded combat power.

**2. The Carrier**
*"Ammunition doesn't walk itself forward. Water doesn't distribute itself. What the formation needs to fight, I bring to the fighting. The logistics are the strategy."*
- **Defining Capability:** The supply load — the physical weight of ammunition, water, power cells, and medical consumables carried forward to the point of need, delivered under the same conditions the formation is fighting in.

**3. The Cutter**
*"We can't save everything. I decide what we save and what we spend. The fire team with three casualties gets the medic; the fire team at full strength holds its sector without resupply. The deciding is the hardest work in the formation."*
- **Defining Capability:** The triage protocol — the classification system that converts unlimited need into prioritized allocation, distributing finite resources across the formation by severity, criticality, and probability of effect.

**4. The Salvager**
*"The destroyed vehicle has parts. The abandoned position has supplies. The battlefield grows resources if you know how to harvest it. I recycle the fight's wreckage into the fight's fuel."*
- **Defining Capability:** The recovery kit — the tools and the eye for stripping usable components from destroyed, abandoned, or captured assets and returning them to the formation's supply chain.

### Modes

1. **Assessment** — How the Steward ↔ Harvester reads the formation's state: identifying what's damaged, what's depleted, what's about to fail, and what can be recovered from the environment before it needs to be requested from the rear.
2. **Intervention** — The active phase of restoring capability: the casualty treated, the weapon repaired, the ammunition distributed, the captured supply integrated. The moment where degradation reverses.
3. **Triage** — Prioritizing limited resources across unlimited need: who gets treated first, what gets repaired first, where resupply goes when there isn't enough. The Cutter's face made into a universal discipline.
4. **Under Fire** — How the role sustains the formation during active contact: treating casualties in the beaten zone, resupplying fighting positions that are still engaged, repairing equipment that is still being shot at.
5. **Exhaustion** — How the role functions when sustainment can no longer keep pace with degradation: the casualty beyond treatment, the ammunition that's gone, the equipment that can't be repaired. The moment where the Steward becomes the Harvester by necessity, or accepts that the formation's capability has contracted permanently.

## Role 6: Paver ↔ Root — *Ground Relationship*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages the formation's relationship with terrain — reading ground, finding routes for movement, preparing positions for occupation, and deciding when the formation moves and when it stays. This role defines whether the formation flows across the battlespace or anchors to it, and ensures that either choice serves the fight.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover what happens at the specific threshold of a contested entry point (that's the Breacher ↔ Warden), the detection of threats along the route or around the position (that's the Feeler ↔ Curtain), or the tactical decision of *why* to move or stay (that's the Caller ↔ Executor). The Paver ↔ Root manages terrain. They don't manage doors, eyes, or decisions.

### Faces

**1. The Guide**
*"I've walked this ground before the formation walks it. The route I've marked is the route that doesn't kill you. Trust the path — the path is my professional opinion."*
- **Defining Capability:** The route reconnaissance and marking system — the physical or digital breadcrumb trail that tells the formation where safe passage exists, generated by a soldier who went first and came back alive.

**2. The Pioneer**
*"There is no route. I make one. Obstacles cleared, hazards marked, crossing points constructed — the formation moves because I moved the ground out of the way first."*
- **Defining Capability:** The engineering toolkit — demolitions, construction materials, obstacle-clearing equipment. The capability that converts impassable terrain into passable terrain through labor, explosives, and professional stubbornness.

**3. The Stake**
*"This position is selected, prepared, and occupied. The formation fights from here. The ground I chose is the ground that gives us the advantage, and I chose it because I read the ground better than the enemy read it."*
- **Defining Capability:** The fighting position — the prepared ground that converts raw terrain into tactical advantage: fields of fire cleared, cover improved, concealment maintained, sustainment planned.

**4. The Pivot**
*"The formation maneuvers around me. I am the reference point that gives the movement coherence. When the formation wheels, it wheels on my position."*
- **Defining Capability:** The fixed point in a mobile fight — the position held not for its own sake but because the formation's maneuver requires a stable axis, and that axis is a person standing on a piece of ground that does not move.

### Modes

1. **Terrain Reading** — How the Paver ↔ Root evaluates ground: slope, cover, concealment, trafficability, observation, fields of fire. The tactical character of the earth, translated into options the formation can act on.
2. **Preparation** — Converting raw terrain into useful terrain: clearing routes, building positions, marking hazards, improving cover. The labor that turns geography into advantage.
3. **Navigation** — Moving the formation through complex ground: maintaining direction, pace, and formation integrity in conditions — darkness, weather, vegetation, urban rubble — that degrade all three.
4. **Occupation** — Establishing and sustaining a position: the transition from "a point on the map" to "a defensible, sustainable location the formation can fight from and live in."
5. **Displacement** — The art of leaving ground: the paver who must abandon the route when the route is compromised, the root who must uproot when the position is untenable. How the role lets go of terrain it has invested in.

## Role 7: Caller ↔ Executor — *Decision Line*

**Operational Definition:** The soldier who manages the boundary between assessment and action — reading the tactical situation to determine what the formation should do next, or translating that determination into physical reality with their own hands and voice. This role is the link between the formation's brain and its body: it decides, and it makes the decision real.

**NOT:** This role does NOT cover the specific delivery of fires (that's the Striker ↔ Absorber), the management of routes and positions (that's the Paver ↔ Root), the detection of threats (that's the Feeler ↔ Curtain), or the sustainment of capability (that's the Steward ↔ Harvester). The Caller ↔ Executor determines what the formation does. They don't do everything themselves — they ensure the doing matches the deciding.

### Faces

**1. The Reader**
*"I see the fight before the fight sees us. The pattern in the enemy's movement, the gap in their coverage, the moment the timing shifts — I read it and I call it before the window closes."*
- **Defining Capability:** The tactical assessment — the mental model of the engagement that converts chaos into options, generated by a brain that has learned to see patterns under fire at the speed of relevance.

**2. The Voice**
*"The plan means nothing if the formation can't hear it. I translate the read into orders clear enough to execute at the speed of contact. Clarity under fire is my weapon."*
- **Defining Capability:** The order — spoken, signaled, or transmitted. The command that converts one person's tactical assessment into twelve people's coordinated action, delivered in language the formation can act on without asking for clarification they don't have time for.

**3. The Adapter**
*"The first plan died on contact. The second plan is dying right now. I read the wreckage and build the third plan from the pieces that survived. I am faster than the enemy's disruption."*
- **Defining Capability:** The fragmentary order — the mid-contact adjustment that realigns the formation's effort with reality as reality changes. The capability to plan at the speed of the fight rather than the speed of the briefing.

**4. The Closer**
*"Someone has to execute the call with their own hands at the point where the fight is decided. The decision was made. I'm the one who makes the decision physical."*
- **Defining Capability:** The personal weapon and the willingness to be at the decisive point — the capability that converts the caller's assessment into fact on the ground, performed by a soldier who closes the gap between the plan and the planet.

### Modes

1. **Reading** — How the Caller ↔ Executor assesses the tactical situation: pattern recognition, threat prioritization, opportunity identification, the synthesis of inputs from every other role into a picture the formation can act on.
2. **Decision** — The moment of commitment: choosing a course of action with incomplete information under time pressure. The irreversible instant where options collapse into intent.
3. **Communication** — How the decision becomes an order the formation can execute: clarity, brevity, authority, the translation of one mind's assessment into a group's coordinated action.
4. **Adaptation** — How the role responds when the decision meets reality and reality disagrees: mid-fight adjustment, the kill of a plan that isn't working, the construction of a new plan from the remains of the old one.
5. **Accountability** — How the role processes outcomes: the call that worked, the call that killed someone, the learning that feeds the next decision. The weight of having been the one who said "go."

## Differentiation Matrix

For any pair of roles that might be confused, here is the line between them:

| Role Pair | Distinction |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Breacher ↔ Warden** vs. **Paver ↔ Root** | The Breacher ↔ Warden manages *contested thresholds* — doors, barriers, chokepoints where access is forced or denied in a moment; the Paver ↔ Root manages *terrain over distance and time* — routes, positions, the ground between thresholds. |
| **Feeler ↔ Curtain** vs. **Relay ↔ Veil** | The Feeler ↔ Curtain *detects threats through physical and sensor observation*; the Relay ↔ Veil *manages the information systems* that carry what's detected and attacks the enemy's equivalent systems. |
| **Striker ↔ Absorber** vs. **Breacher ↔ Warden** | The Striker ↔ Absorber applies or absorbs kinetic force *across the breadth of an engagement*; the Breacher ↔ Warden applies force *at a specific access point* to control passage, not to win a firefight. |
| **Caller ↔ Executor** vs. **Relay ↔ Veil** | The Caller ↔ Executor *makes decisions and drives execution*; the Relay ↔ Veil *transmits and protects the information* those decisions depend on, without making the decisions themselves. |
| **Steward ↔ Harvester** vs. **Striker ↔ Absorber** | The Steward ↔ Harvester *restores and preserves* the formation's capacity to fight across time; the Striker ↔ Absorber *applies and endures* violence within the current engagement. |
| **Paver ↔ Root** vs. **Feeler ↔ Curtain** | The Paver ↔ Root manages the formation's *physical relationship with terrain* — routes, positions, ground; the Feeler ↔ Curtain manages the formation's *perceptual relationship with threats* — detection, identification, surveillance. |
| **Caller ↔ Executor** vs. **Paver ↔ Root** | The Caller ↔ Executor decides *what* the formation does and ensures it happens; the Paver ↔ Root determines *where* the formation does it by reading and preparing the ground. |
| **Breacher ↔ Warden** vs. **Striker ↔ Absorber** vs. **Paver ↔ Root** | Three roles all touch "force meets terrain" — the Breacher opens/closes *a single point*, the Striker projects/absorbs fire *across an area*, and the Paver prepares/holds *the ground itself*. The threshold, the fire, and the earth. |
| **Feeler ↔ Curtain** vs. **Caller ↔ Executor** | The Feeler ↔ Curtain *finds and reports*; the Caller ↔ Executor *decides and directs*. One generates the picture; the other acts on it. |
| **Steward ↔ Harvester** vs. **Paver ↔ Root** | The Steward ↔ Harvester sustains the formation's *people and equipment*; the Paver ↔ Root sustains the formation's *relationship with the ground*. Capability versus position. |

## Source Mapping

Each role draws from the source framework's 7 factors, but specific factors and faces shaped each role's design more than others:

### Role 1: Breacher ↔ Warden
- **Primary Source — Factor 5: Assault × Occupation.** The Breach's kinetic entry philosophy ("The mission has a door. I open the door.") directly informs the Breacher faces. The Holder's position-security mentality ("I make the building safe enough to live in") directly informs the Warden faces.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 2: Proximity × Distance.** The Point's acceptance of close-range engagement at thresholds, and the Overwatch's standoff denial capability, map to the Breacher's need to be at the door and the Warden's ability to deny from depth.
- **Face Mapping:** The Key and Ram descend from The Breach and The Point. The Lock and Vault descend from The Holder and The Dirtside's terrain-as-fortification.

### Role 2: Feeler ↔ Curtain
- **Primary Source — Factor 2: Proximity × Distance.** The Overwatch's long-range observation ("I see them before they see my people") and The Point's close reconnaissance both feed the Feeler's detection capability. The Drone's remote sensing informs the Web face's sensor-system architecture.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 3: Gravity × Vacuum.** The Dirtside's terrain reading ("reads and uses planetary terrain") informs the Feeler's environmental awareness. The Jumper's environmental transition capability informs the Feeler's ability to operate across domains.
- **Face Mapping:** The Whisker descends from The Point's close observation. The Hound descends from The Hunter's compulsive seeking (redirected from killing to finding). The Post descends from The Overwatch's vigilant standoff. The Web descends from The Linked's network awareness.

### Role 3: Striker ↔ Absorber
- **Primary Source — Factor 2: Proximity × Distance.** The full axis of engagement range — from The Point's room-clearing to The Overwatch's long-range precision — defines how the Striker delivers and the Absorber positions.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 4: Expendability × Worth.** The Counted's value-aware risk calculation and The Spent's willingness to absorb cost directly shape the Absorber faces. The Round's self-as-ammunition philosophy represents the Striker at terminal commitment.
- **Tertiary Source — Factor 1: System × Flesh.** The Linked's targeting integration and The Nerve's human judgment both inform how the Striker acquires and the Absorber reads incoming threat.
- **Face Mapping:** The Hammer descends from The Breach's kinetic philosophy. The Needle descends from The Overwatch's precision standoff. The Rampart descends from The Counted's calculated self-exposure and The Holder's position absorption. The Smoke descends from The Jumper's environmental manipulation.

### Role 4: Relay ↔ Veil
- **Primary Source — Factor 1: System × Flesh.** The Linked's network capabilities ("Has become the squad's central nervous system"), data-sharing modes, and human-machine interface directly define the Relay's function. The Nerve's ability to provide irreducibly human signal reading provides the counterweight.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 6: Autonomy × Swarm.** The Sync's distributed coordination architecture ("twelve sensors, twelve weapons, and a distributed processor") directly informs the Bridge and Filter faces. The Singleton's independent signal processing informs the Veil's ability to operate when the network is degraded.
- **Face Mapping:** The Bridge descends from The Linked's Relay mode and The Sync's collective architecture. The Filter descends from The Linked's curated data-sharing. The Ghost descends from The Drone's digital abstraction (redirected toward the enemy). The Mirror descends from The Linked's Corrupted Override mode (redirected outward as deception rather than inward as control).

### Role 5: Steward ↔ Harvester
- **Primary Source — Factor 4: Expendability × Worth.** The entire axis of human value and expendability — The Counted's realistic self-worth, The Spent's honest acceptance of cost, The Precious's inflated self-protection, The Round's self-as-ammunition — defines the moral landscape the Steward ↔ Harvester operates in. Every triage decision is a statement about worth.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 7: Continuity × Displacement.** The Continuous's preservation of identity through disruption ("I bring thread") directly informs the Steward's mandate to maintain the formation across gaps. The Adapted's ability to function in temporal dislocation informs the Harvester's ability to salvage capability from changed conditions.
- **Tertiary Source — Factor 5: Assault × Occupation.** The Aftermath's awareness of what assault costs and The Holder's long-term sustainment mentality both feed the Steward's responsibility to manage consequence.
- **Face Mapping:** The Mender descends from The Continuous's preservation instinct. The Carrier descends from The Spent's acceptance of burden. The Cutter descends from The Counted's realistic value assessment. The Salvager descends from The Adapted's ability to extract function from displacement.

### Role 6: Paver ↔ Root
- **Primary Source — Factor 3: Gravity × Vacuum.** The Dirtside's terrain mastery ("reads and uses planetary terrain," "the entrenching tool and the fighting position") directly defines the Root faces. The Jumper's environmental transition capability ("lives in the transition") directly defines the Paver's ability to navigate across domains.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 5: Assault × Occupation.** The Holder's position selection and governance ("the patrol route and the handshake") informs the Root's occupation modes. The Breach's forward momentum informs the Paver's route-finding.
- **Tertiary Source — Factor 3: Gravity × Vacuum (shadow).** The Buried's pathological attachment to ground and The Untethered's inability to anchor both inform the failure modes of the Root and Paver respectively.
- **Face Mapping:** The Guide descends from The Jumper's transition expertise. The Pioneer descends from The Dirtside's terrain manipulation. The Stake descends from The Holder's occupation capability. The Pivot descends from The Dirtside's gravity-as-anchor philosophy.

### Role 7: Caller ↔ Executor
- **Primary Source — Factor 6: Autonomy × Swarm.** The Singleton's independent judgment under fire ("My gut carries one: right or wrong") directly informs the Reader and Adapter faces. The Sync's collective coordination ("I think with the formation. The formation thinks with me.") informs the Voice and Closer faces where decisions must become collective action.
- **Secondary Source — Factor 1: System × Flesh.** The Nerve's trust in human judgment over machine data and The Linked's ability to leverage system capability both inform how the Caller reads the fight — through gut, through data, or through both held in tension.
- **Tertiary Source — Factor 4: Expendability × Worth.** The Counted's ability to weigh cost against purpose informs the Caller's accountability mode — the weight of having sent people into the decision's consequences.
